SAVINGS & GROWTH GUIDE

What is compound interest?

Compound growth means that returns can themselves contribute to future growth. Over long periods, the effect can become much larger than simple growth on the original amount alone.

The four inputs that matter

Starting balance, regular contributions, annual rate and time are the main drivers. Contribution frequency and compounding frequency can also change the illustration.

Why time matters

Growth is applied repeatedly. That means a longer period can have a much larger effect even when the rate stays the same. But a projected rate is only an assumption: savings rates change and investment returns are not guaranteed.

Keep contributions realistic and treat any growth projection as a scenario rather than a promise.
