WorthChex guides are designed to explain the numbers behind common financial decisions without pretending there is one answer for everyone.

How do I know if I can afford a car?

Look beyond the purchase price. Consider the monthly payment if financed, insurance, fuel, maintenance, tax where applicable and the rest of your budget.

Should I pay cash or use finance?

Compare the upfront cash impact with the ongoing monthly commitment. Also consider APR, fees, term length and how much breathing room remains.

How much emergency savings should I have?

Your target depends on essential spending, income stability and your personal circumstances. A useful buffer should be realistic enough to maintain.

How much should I save each month?

Start with an amount you can maintain consistently. A smaller regular contribution is often more useful than a target you repeatedly miss.

How long does it take to save £10,000?

Subtract what you already have from the target and divide what remains by your monthly contribution.

What is disposable income?

It is the money left after the spending you've included in the calculation. It is useful for planning, but it won't automatically include every future cost.

What should I include in a monthly budget?

Include the recurring costs that actually apply to you: housing, utilities, food, transport, subscriptions, debt payments and other regular commitments.

How does debt interest affect repayment?

Interest is added before the payment reduces the balance. A higher payment can therefore shorten the timeline and reduce interest under the simplified model.

There is no universal safe percentage. A useful test is whether your remaining money still gives you room for irregular costs and unexpected expenses.
