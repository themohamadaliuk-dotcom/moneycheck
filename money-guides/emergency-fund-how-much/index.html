How much emergency savings should I have?

There is no single number that works for everyone. A useful emergency fund is large enough to help with essential costs and disruptions, but realistic enough that you can actually build it.

Start with essential spending

Estimate the costs you would still need to cover if your income stopped: housing, utilities, food, transport, insurance, minimum debt payments and other essentials. Avoid treating every optional expense as essential.

Then choose a target

Your target can reflect job stability, income variability, dependants, health or other personal circumstances. A household with one steady income may choose a different buffer from someone with irregular self-employed earnings.

It can help to choose a first milestone rather than waiting for one very large number. Build a small buffer first, then increase it over time.

Use the calculator as a planning check

Enter your current savings and emergency-fund target in the Affordability Calculator to see how a purchase could affect your buffer. You can also build a target using the Savings Goal Calculator.
