METHODOLOGY

How WorthChex calculates its results.

WorthChex aims to make the logic behind its calculators understandable. The results are estimates based on the figures entered and should not be treated as personalised financial advice.

AFFORDABILITY

Affordability calculation

Disposable income

Disposable income = monthly income − entered monthly expenses

Only expense fields actually filled in by the user are included. Blank categories are excluded rather than assumed to be £0.

Cash purchase

Money after purchase = disposable income − purchase price

This represents the effect of paying the full purchase price from the monthly budget represented by the entries.

Financed purchase

Money after finance = disposable income − monthly finance payment

For a financed purchase, WorthChex uses the monthly payment when assessing the ongoing monthly commitment instead of subtracting the entire purchase price from one month.

Savings check

Savings after purchase = current savings − purchase price

This is shown as a separate hypothetical check. Savings do not automatically make a purchase more or less affordable.

SAVINGS

Savings goal calculation

Remaining amount

Remaining goal = savings goal − current savings

Estimated months

Months = remaining goal ÷ monthly saving

The result is rounded up to the next whole month.

Important assumptions

The calculation does not assume interest, investment returns, withdrawals, missed contributions or changes to the monthly saving amount.

DEBT

Debt payoff calculation

Monthly interest

Monthly interest = remaining balance × APR ÷ 12

Principal reduction

Principal = monthly payment − monthly interest

Payoff timeline

The calculator applies the simplified monthly calculation repeatedly until the balance reaches approximately zero.

If the payment is no greater than the interest being added, the balance will not fall under this model.

LIMITATIONS

What the calculators don't know

WorthChex cannot automatically know about irregular spending, future changes in income, taxes, fees, future commitments, variable interest rates, lender-specific policies or your personal priorities.

The calculator should therefore be viewed as a planning tool rather than a definitive answer to a financial decision.
